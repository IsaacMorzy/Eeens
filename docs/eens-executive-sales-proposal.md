## 1. Executive decision

Eens has the foundations of a useful digital operating system: a factual public property register, an operational Frappe and ERPNext site, customer relationship management, rental and utility billing, a communication layer, and a permission-controlled assistant layer.

The commercial opportunity is to connect those foundations around one operating truth:

> **Every published property, enquiry, viewing, lease, meter reading, invoice, payment, and follow-up should be traceable to the same property and customer relationship.**

Nimbuz Tech proposes a six-month programme to help Eens:

- publish verified property opportunities with greater confidence;
- turn search, social, advertising, and conversations into owned enquiries;
- respond to prospects consistently and route each opportunity to the right person;
- move qualified interest through viewing, offer, contract, rent, and utility operations;
- measure marketing by qualified demand, occupancy, collections, and revenue rather than reach alone;
- give managers faster answers from controlled operational data without bypassing permissions.

This is an evidence-led proposal. It distinguishes what is installed today from what must be configured, integrated, tested, and approved.

## 2. Why this matters to Eens

Eens operates across connected property markets:

- industrial and commercial space in Mlolongo, Syokimau, Baba Dogo, and the wider Mombasa Road corridor;
- godowns, warehouses, business-park units, retail space, and related commercial opportunities;
- residential opportunities in Thika and other approved locations.

These markets compete for attention, but the operating questions are shared:

1. What is available, where is it, and what are the verified terms?
2. Who is asking, what do they need, and when do they need it?
3. Which enquiries become viewings, offers, contracts, and collected revenue?
4. Which locations and campaigns produce useful demand?
5. Which leases, meters, invoices, payments, renewals, and service issues need attention?

The programme is designed to answer those questions with one connected customer and property journey.

### Expected business effects

| Business area | Expected improvement |
|---|---|
| Occupancy | Faster response, fewer stale listings, clearer viewing queues, and stronger follow-up |
| Leasing | A consistent path from enquiry to property match, viewing, offer, contract, and invoice |
| Billing | Less manual reconciliation across rent, utility consumption, adjustments, and receivables |
| Marketing | Clear evidence of which channels and content create qualified property conversations |
| Customer service | Conversations and requests remain connected to the relevant customer and property context |
| Management | Availability, tenancy, pipeline, collections, utility, and service signals in one reporting rhythm |

| Trust | Public pages show clear facts, explicit availability, listing references, and a reliable next action |

## 5. The growth-to-occupancy strategy

### Demand generation

Create useful demand around specific locations, property types, sizes, terms, and verified opportunities through:

- organic search and location pages;
- Google Business Profile activity where ownership and eligibility are verified;
- practical articles answering real leasing and property questions;
- short property and location videos using approved imagery;
- social distribution and community-facing content;
- targeted paid campaigns funded directly by Eens.

### Demand capture

Give every interested prospect a clear next step:

- request a viewing;
- ask about a named listing;
- request a specification pack;
- describe a size, zone, use, budget, or move-in requirement;
- request a callback;
- ask about an apartment or commercial unit.

The resulting enquiry should retain its source, campaign, landing page, property reference, consent state, and next action.

### Commercial follow-through

A qualified prospect moves through a consistent human-led process:

```text
Search / social / referral / advertising
                ↓
Public property page or conversation
                ↓
Enquiry captured with source and consent
                ↓
Lead owner and response target assigned
                ↓
Needs qualified and viewing arranged
                ↓
Property/service request and commercial opportunity
                ↓
Contract, deposit, rent, utilities, and customer care
                ↓
Revenue, renewal, feedback, and better content
```

This makes marketing accountable to the property operation. It also gives leasing and operations a clearer view of demand before it is lost in disconnected inboxes.

## 6. SEO and digital marketing operating system

### Search architecture

The public register should grow around useful search intent, not generic campaign language:

- godowns and warehouses for rent in approved locations;
- warehouse space on the Mombasa Road corridor;
- shops and commercial space at approved business-park locations;
- property specifications by size, power, access, floor loading, clear height, water, and terms;
- location pages, lease guidance, viewing information, and recurring customer questions;
- residential pages built only from approved locations and verified records.

Every page should have one clear purpose, one canonical URL, visible property facts, a useful next action, and a way to measure the resulting enquiry.

### Local search

For each verified Eens operating location, the workstream should cover:

- accurate ownership and business information;
- correct category, address, hours, contact routing, and landing page;
- useful images and factual updates;
- review requests that follow consent and service policy;
- response tracking from profile activity to enquiry and viewing.

Profile duplication or unsupported location claims should not be used as a growth shortcut.

### Content operations

The programme should maintain a practical publishing rhythm:

- one useful location or specification article each month;
- availability updates when approved records change;
- short video or image sets for verified locations or units;
- answer pages based on repeated CRM questions;
- a monthly review of search demand, qualified enquiries, viewings, and outcomes.

Content is accepted when it has a source, a customer question or target query, a defined next action, and a link to an approved property or contact route.

## 7. Data and management visibility

The programme should collect only data that supports a business decision or a customer response.

### Demand data

- source, campaign, landing page, and listing reference;
- consent, contact details, requirement, location, size, use, budget band, and timeline;
- owner, response target, status, next action, viewing outcome, offer state, and lost reason;
- conversion to customer, service request, contract, invoice, payment, and renewal.

### Management views

1. **Availability:** available, reserved, occupied, maintenance, unpublished, and review age.
2. **Leasing funnel:** enquiries, qualified opportunities, viewings, offers, contracts, and response time.
3. **Source performance:** organic, local search, paid, social, referral, and direct conversion.
4. **Receivables:** due, overdue, ageing, deposits, collections, and exceptions.
5. **Tenancy:** active contracts, renewal dates, escalation dates, notices, and service status.
6. **Utilities:** consumption, meter exceptions, tariff blocks, abnormal usage, and billing state.
7. **Content:** indexed pages, landing-page enquiries, listing freshness, and content gaps.

Reports should distinguish reach, interest, quality, commercial outcome, and retention. Traffic is not treated as success unless it produces a useful property conversation or business result.

## 8. AI integration for better decisions

The assistant layer should reduce search and reporting time while keeping human accountability.

The first release should help approved users answer questions such as:

- Which approved units match a location and size requirement?
- Which listings need review?
- Which leads have no next action?
- What happened to this month’s viewings by source?
- Which contracts approach renewal?
- Which properties show unusual utility consumption?
- Which collection follow-ups need attention?
- What are the key dates and obligations in an approved document?

The assistant should begin with read, explain, compare, and prepare actions. Public claims, financial submissions, availability changes, bulk messages, and destructive actions require role permissions, explicit confirmation, and an audit record.

## 9. Six-month programme

### Phase 0 — Discovery and agreement, weeks 1–2

- Confirm property vocabulary, owners, locations, data boundaries, consent, and KPIs.
- Agree the operational source of truth and public publishing rules.
- Confirm roles, acceptance owners, provider onboarding, and security responsibilities.

**Outcome:** approved data dictionary, responsibility map, and delivery contract.

### Phase 1 — Operational foundation, weeks 3–6

- Configure property, customer, billing, CRM, role, and approval conventions.
- Prepare the controlled integration surface in `eens_app`.
- Establish test data and management reporting definitions.

**Outcome:** a test property can move through a controlled service and billing preview.

### Phase 2 — Public publishing and SEO, weeks 5–8

- Define approved listing fields and review workflow.
- Connect public publishing to verified operational review.
- Implement metadata, sitemap, structured-data, landing-page, and attribution standards.

**Outcome:** an approved availability change can be published and traced to a listing reference.

### Phase 3 — Lead and conversation operations, weeks 7–10

- Configure public enquiry routes and approved communication channels.
- Route enquiries into CRM with ownership, response targets, consent, source, and property context.
- Add viewing and follow-up workflows.

**Outcome:** a test enquiry becomes one owned, traceable commercial record.

### Phase 4 — Finance and tenant operations, weeks 9–14

- Configure contracts, deposits, recurring rent, meters, tariffs, invoices, and exception handling.
- Integrate one approved payment method after provider onboarding and reconciliation tests.

**Outcome:** a test tenancy completes the agreed contract-to-invoice-to-payment path.

### Phase 5 — Assistant and management intelligence, weeks 12–18

- Add Eens-specific read-only skills and tools.
- Build the management pack and verify answers against source reports.
- Add approval gates for any write action.

**Outcome:** approved managers receive permission-correct, auditable answers.

### Phase 6 — Growth and optimisation, weeks 16–24

- Run measured SEO, local search, content, social, email, and paid experiments.
- Improve stale inventory, lost-lead, response, renewal, and collection workflows.
- Complete handover, security review, backup test, and operating runbook.

**Outcome:** a repeatable growth and property-operations cadence.

## 10. Proposed success measures

Baseline values should be captured during Phase 0. Targets should be agreed after the baseline rather than invented in advance.

| Measure | Business meaning |
|---|---|
| First response time | How quickly Eens acknowledges useful demand |
| Lead completeness | Whether the team has the facts needed to act |
| Qualified lead rate | Whether marketing creates relevant enquiries |
| Viewing rate | Whether qualified demand reaches a property visit |
| Lease conversion | Whether opportunities become signed contracts |
| Source-to-revenue | Which channels produce commercial value |
| Listing freshness | Whether public information can be trusted |
| Occupancy | How much approved operational inventory is occupied |
| Collection rate | How much value due is collected in the period |
| Renewal coverage | Whether upcoming renewals have an owned next action |

## 11. Commercial framework

The following structure is indicative and should be confirmed after Phase 0:

- **Proposed service fee:** KSh 120,000 per month for six months.
- **Media budget:** paid directly by Eens to selected advertising platforms.
- **Third-party charges:** hosting, messaging, payment-provider, verification, domain, and paid SaaS charges are excluded unless expressly included in the signed scope.
- **Client responsibilities:** timely access, accurate property records, approved imagery, provider onboarding, legal/privacy approval, and named owners for leasing, finance, operations, marketing, and technology.

The service fee covers only the agreed engineering, SEO, marketing, reporting, training, and maintenance scope. It does not imply unlimited content, unlimited advertising spend, or 24/7 incident response without a separate SLA.
